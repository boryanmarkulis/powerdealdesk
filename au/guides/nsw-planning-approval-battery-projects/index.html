NSW planning approval process for battery projects

In NSW, larger battery projects are usually assessed as State Significant Development (electricity generating works above the capital investment threshold in the Planning Systems SEPP). The path is a scoping report, SEARs (assessment requirements), an EIS, public exhibition, response to submissions, department assessment, then determination by the Minister, delegate or the Independent Planning Commission. Smaller projects go through council.

Stages

  1. Scoping report and request for SEARs
  2. SEARs issued (they can lapse if no EIS is lodged in time)
  3. EIS prepared and lodged
  4. Public exhibition and submissions
  5. Response to submissions
  6. Assessment report by the Department
  7. Determination; the IPC decides where specific triggers (e.g. objection numbers or council objection) apply

Status of each SSD is public on the NSW Planning Portal. See NSW projects in our NSW tracker.

FAQ

Where can I check a NSW battery project's approval status?

On the NSW Planning Portal major projects register, searchable by name or SSD number.

When does the IPC decide?

When triggers in the planning rules apply, such as a large number of unique objections or an objection from the local council.

Do small batteries need SSD approval?

No; below the SSD threshold they are generally assessed by local council.
