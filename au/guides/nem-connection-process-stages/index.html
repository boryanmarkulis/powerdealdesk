NEM connection process stages explained

Connecting a battery to the NEM follows the National Electricity Rules Chapter 5 process: a connection enquiry to the network service provider, a connection application with detailed models, negotiation and AEMO assessment of generator performance standards (the "5.3.4A" step), a signed connection agreement, then construction, AEMO registration and staged commissioning (hold points).

Stages

  1. Pre-feasibility / enquiry: the network service provider (NSP) responds with preliminary information and requirements.
  2. Application: proponent submits an application with plant data and power-system models.
  3. Performance standards (NER 5.3.4A): proposed access standards are negotiated; AEMO advises on standards within its remit. Approval letters are a key de-risking milestone for buyers.
  4. Connection agreement: offer and execution with the NSP.
  5. Registration and commissioning: AEMO registration, then testing through hold points until full output.

Projects in Renewable Energy Zones may follow separate access schemes. See ready-to-build checklist.

FAQ

What is a 5.3.4A letter?

Shorthand for approval of negotiated generator performance standards under NER clause 5.3.4A, a major connection milestone.

Who runs the connection process?

The local network service provider, with AEMO involved in assessing performance standards and registration.

How long does connection take?

It varies widely by project and network; there is no fixed timeline.
