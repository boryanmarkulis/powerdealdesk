How to buy a battery storage project in Australia

To buy a battery storage project in Australia, identify development-stage projects (AEMO Generation Information and state planning portals are the public starting points), approach the owner, then diligence planning consent, grid connection stage, land rights and offtake path, and agree a share or asset sale, often with milestone-based payments. Foreign buyers may need FIRB approval.

1. Source projects

Most early-stage NEM projects appear in AEMO's Generation Information as "Publicly Announced" or "Anticipated". State planning portals show consent status. Many sales are bilateral rather than run as auctions.

2. Diligence the value drivers

  • Planning: is development consent granted, and on what conditions and lapse dates?
  • Connection: how far through the network service provider and AEMO connection process is the project (e.g. has a 5.3.4A letter been issued)?
  • Land: option or lease, term and easements.
  • Revenue: arbitrage, FCAS, tolling or government tenders such as the Capacity Investment Scheme.
  • Environment: EPBC referral need.

3. Structure

Deals are usually a share sale of the project SPV or an asset sale, with upfront and milestone payments (e.g. at connection agreement or financial close). See the ready-to-build checklist.

FAQ

Where can I find battery projects for sale in Australia?

There is no single official sale register. Public sources are AEMO Generation Information and state planning portals; our tracker compiles them.

Do foreign investors need approval to buy an Australian energy project?

Possibly. Acquisitions by foreign persons can require FIRB approval depending on thresholds and asset type; check foreigninvestment.gov.au and get advice.

What stage is cheapest to buy?

Earlier-stage projects cost less but carry planning and connection risk; ready-to-build projects command higher prices.
