Capacity Investment Scheme (CIS) explained for BESS and storage

The Capacity Investment Scheme (CIS) is an Australian Government revenue-underwriting program that runs competitive tenders for long-term Capacity Investment Scheme Agreements (CISAs). Successful renewable generation (e.g. wind and solar) and clean dispatchable projects (e.g. battery storage) can receive partial revenue support after they are built and fully operational, reducing wholesale-price and offtake risk so more capacity can reach financial close.

What the CIS is

The CIS is a Commonwealth program to accelerate investment in new renewable generation and clean dispatchable capacity (including batteries). According to the tender delivery partner ASL, the scheme aims to deliver 40 GW of new capacity nationally — 26 GW of renewable generation and 14 GW of clean dispatchable capacity — supporting the Government's 82% renewable electricity by 2030 target, reliability as coal exits, and downward pressure on prices.

It is a revenue safety net, not a construction grant. The Government does not pay to build the project; support is available only after the project is built and fully operating.

Who runs it

  • Australian Government (policy owner; signs CISAs with successful proponents).
  • Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water (DCCEEW) publishes scheme policy, open tenders and progress pages.
  • AusEnergy Services Limited (ASL) administers the competitive tender process and recommends bids on a merit basis (with AEMO engaged alongside ASL as tender delivery partners, per DCCEEW).

Always use the current Open CIS tenders page and the matching ASL tender page for dates, capacity targets and guidelines — tender rounds change.

How a tender works

  1. Guidelines published on ASL for that round (eligibility, merit criteria, draft CISA, capacity target).
  2. Bids assessed against those published eligibility and merit criteria (ASL describes a single-stage process for recent rounds).
  3. Outcomes announced; the Government then negotiates with successful proponents.
  4. CISA signed if negotiations complete satisfactorily.

DCCEEW describes bidding as a competitive reverse-auction style process: proponents bid the support terms they need. Merit weightings typically include deliverability, system benefits, and community / First Nations engagement — read each round's guidelines rather than assuming one template.

How underwriting (the CISA) works

A Capacity Investment Scheme Agreement (CISA) is a contract between the project proponent and the Australian Government. ASL summarises the commercial idea as follows (exact definitions sit in each proforma CISA):

  • Proponents bid an Annual Floor, Annual Ceiling and Annual Payment Cap (among other bid variables).
  • If project revenue in a support year falls below the Annual Floor, the Commonwealth tops up 90% of the shortfall (subject to the Annual Payment Cap).
  • If revenue rises above the Annual Ceiling, the project pays 50% of the excess to the Commonwealth (also subject to the cap).
  • Support runs only while the project is fully operational and generating revenue; DCCEEW notes underwriting can last from 1 to 15 years, with the end date a bid variable in tender guidelines.

Before support starts, CISA holders typically report milestones such as land tenure, planning and environmental approvals, grid connection offer, financial close, construction and operation.

Why CIS matters for BESS / storage

Clean dispatchable tenders are the CIS path most relevant to standalone batteries and hybrid storage. A CISA can make merchant or partially contracted BESS revenue stacks bankable by capping downside wholesale and offtake risk — which is why CIS status is a diligence item for buyers (see how to buy a battery project and the ready-to-build checklist).

Winning a tender is not the same as being built: connection, planning and construction still have to clear. Conversely, many NEM batteries proceed without CIS support.

FAQ

Is the Capacity Investment Scheme a grant to build batteries?

No. CIS is revenue underwriting. DCCEEW states the scheme does not pay to build or operate projects; support is only available after a project is built and fully operating.

Who assesses CIS tenders?

ASL administers the competitive tender process and recommends bids to the Australian Government against each round's published eligibility and merit criteria. AEMO and ASL are engaged as tender delivery partners (per DCCEEW). The Commonwealth decides and signs CISAs.

What is a CISA?

A Capacity Investment Scheme Agreement — a contract between a successful proponent and the Australian Government for partial revenue support after the project is operational, on terms bid in the tender (including floor, ceiling and payment cap).

How does the floor and ceiling work?

Under published ASL tender guidelines for recent dispatchable rounds, if revenue is below the agreed Annual Floor the Commonwealth tops up 90% of the shortfall; if revenue exceeds the Annual Ceiling the project pays 50% of the excess — both subject to an Annual Payment Cap. Exact definitions are in that round's proforma CISA.

Does CIS apply only to the NEM?

No. CIS tenders have been run for both the National Electricity Market (NEM) and Western Australia's Wholesale Electricity Market (WEM). Check DCCEEW's open tenders page for the current rounds.

Where can I see open CIS tenders and past outcomes?

Start at DCCEEW's Capacity Investment Scheme and Open CIS tenders pages, then the matching ASL tender pages for guidelines and draft CISAs. Do not rely on unofficial summaries for bid deadlines.

How does CIS status affect buying a BESS project?

A signed or likely CISA can change the revenue story and bankability, but buyers still diligence planning, connection, land and construction. See our buy guide and ready-to-build checklist, and track public project status in the NEM BESS tracker.
