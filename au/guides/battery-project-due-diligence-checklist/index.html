Battery project due diligence checklist: 12 public-record checks

Before you approach the owner of an Australian battery project, check the public record: AEMO commitment status and survey date, connection record (KCI ID), expected full commercial use date, MW and MWh, site owner and parent company, state planning consent and lapse date, EPBC referral, and changes since the last AEMO release. Every gap becomes a due diligence question.

This is a pre-offer screen from public sources. It is not legal, technical or financial due diligence, which needs advisers and the owner's data room. For the items an owner must prove before closing, use the ready-to-build checklist.

A. AEMO record (Generation Information)

  1. Commitment status. Publicly Announced, Anticipated, Committed* or Committed. See AEMO commitment status explained.
  2. Survey latest update date. Anticipated status lapses without a survey in six months. 179 of 518 early-stage NEM battery sites last updated before 1 April 2026.
  3. KCI ID. AEMO's ID in its Key Connection Information spreadsheet. 383 of 518 early-stage battery sites list one; for the other 135, ask where the connection application stands.
  4. Expected full commercial use date. 314 of 518 early-stage sites list one. Full commercial use needs all NER 5.7.3 commissioning tests signed off by AEMO and the network.
  5. MW and MWh. Duration (MWh ÷ MW) drives revenue options. 124 of 602 sites list no MWh.

B. Ownership

  1. AEMO site owner vs company records. AEMO lists the organisation that financially owns the project. Match it to the ASIC register and find the parent. Owners with 3 or more sites have owner pages.
  2. Owner changes. Compare AEMO releases. A recent owner change can mean a deal already happened; see what changed since July 2026 and disclosed BESS transactions.

C. Approvals

  1. State planning consent. Search the state register (NSW: the Planning Portal major projects register; see the NSW planning guide).
  2. Consent conditions and lapse date. Read the determination, not only the status.
  3. EPBC referral. Search the EPBC Act Public Portal by project name.

D. Grid, land and revenue

  1. Connection stage. Enquiry, application, 5.3.4A performance standards, connection agreement. See NEM connection stages.
  2. Land and revenue path. Land rights for the site and connection easements (both are questions in AEMO's own survey), plus offtake or government tenders such as the Capacity Investment Scheme. Foreign buyers: check FIRB.

FAQ

How is this different from the ready-to-build checklist?

This list screens a project from public records before you contact the owner. The ready-to-build checklist covers what must be secured before a project can close and build.

Can I check a battery project's planning status online?

Yes for most large projects: state planning registers (in NSW, the Planning Portal major projects register) show stage and documents. EPBC referrals are on the EPBC Act Public Portal.

Does PowerDealDesk do legal or technical due diligence?

No. We screen public records and sell single-project reports and Pro research. We do not give legal, technical, financial or investment advice.

What is a KCI ID?

The ID AEMO uses for a project in its Key Connection Information spreadsheet, published on the Generation Information page. It links the generation listing to AEMO's connection information.
